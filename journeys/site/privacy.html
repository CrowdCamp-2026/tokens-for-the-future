What happens to your details, and what we owe you

Tokens of the Future is a small tool built at CrowdCamp 2026 for HCOMP + CI 2026. This week you are the crowd and we are your requesters. This page says what the tool collects, what we promise in return, and what you can do about it.

An independent project by CrowdCamp 2026 participants. It is not an official app of HCOMP + CI 2026 or SIGCHI. The official program is at programs.sigchi.org.

  • Your details stay with the CrowdCamp team. Nothing you give us is sold or handed to sponsors, conference organizers or anyone else.
  • Other participants see only your worker name. You get a pseudonym at sign-up, like “Calibrated Cartographer”, and can change it. Notes are shown to signed-in participants only. Your name, affiliation and email or phone number are seen by the CrowdCamp team only.

Who is responsible

Brian McInnis, Thomas Maillart and Tony Li, who run this CrowdCamp 2026 project. Ask any of us in person at the conference, or write to [email to add before launch].

The site and its database are hosted by Cloudflare.

What we owe you as requesters

Crowd workers wrote down what they expect from academic requesters (the Dynamo guidelines; Salehi et al., We Are Dynamo, CHI 2015). We hold ourselves to the same:

  • Who we are. The three of us, named above, and you can find us at the conference.
  • What the task is for. A report or paper on the future of crowd work, and the topic pages you see this week.
  • How long it takes. A note takes one to three minutes. Only the first field is required, and you choose how many talks to note.
  • What you get. There is no pay. You get a route through the conference, and each topic page shows back what the crowd found.
  • No rejected work. We hide a note only when it attacks a person or shares personal details about someone. Criticism of ideas stays.
  • You can stop at any time and erase your details yourself.

What this is for

The tool plans a route through the conference around one of thirteen questions and collects participants’ notes on the talks: comments, questions, criticisms, how each talk relates to the question, and when people think they will live in that future.

The CrowdCamp team may analyze the notes and quote them in a report or paper about the future of crowd work. Quotes are published without names, affiliations or pseudonyms.

What is collected

  • Your name and affiliation. Seen by the CrowdCamp team only. Other participants see your pseudonym instead.
  • Your pseudonym, chosen at random when you sign up. You can change it in “Your details”. Shown next to the notes you post, including earlier ones when you change it.
  • Your email address or phone number. Used to sign you back in, and to contact you if you ticked the follow-up box. Never shown on the site. Within the team, only the lead organizer can see it.
  • Your notes and answers: the text you write, the kind of note, how the talk relates to your topic, and your answer on timing.
  • A keyed hash of your IP address, never the address itself. It limits how many notes arrive from one network in a few minutes, and it is shown to no one.
  • Your country as reported by the network, used to preselect the phone country code.
  • A session cookie that keeps you signed in for up to five days, and a pass cookie that remembers you opened the participant link. Both are strictly necessary and do no tracking.
  • On your device only: the talks you star. This stays in your browser and never reaches us.

What is not collected

No analytics, no advertising, no tracking pixels and no social-media buttons. The page loads its typefaces from Google Fonts, which sees your IP address when it serves them.

Be courteous and constructive

Speakers and fellow participants read the notes. Engage with the ideas, say what would make the work stronger, and leave out personal details about other people.

Your choices

  • Erase your details yourself, at any time, from “Your details” once signed in. Your name, affiliation, pseudonym and contact are deleted and cannot be recovered. Your notes stay on the site as “Anonymous”.
  • Sign out on a shared device from the same page.
  • Ask the team to remove a note, or anything else, at the contact above.

How long it is kept

Names, affiliations and contacts are deleted by 31 March 2027. Notes are kept after that without names, for the report or paper. Cloudflare keeps database backups for up to 30 days, so erased details leave the backups within 30 days.
